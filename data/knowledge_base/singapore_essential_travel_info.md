# Singapore Essential Travel Information

## Getting to Singapore

### Changi Airport
Singapore Changi Airport (SIN) is consistently voted the world's best airport. It has four terminals (T1-T4) and the Jewel Changi Airport complex. The airport is located in the eastern part of Singapore, about 20 kilometres from the city centre.

**Getting from Changi Airport to the city:**
- **MRT (Mass Rapid Transit):** The East-West Line and Thomson-East Coast Line serve the airport. Journey to City Hall takes about 30 minutes and costs around SGD 2.00.
- **Public Bus:** Bus 36 runs to the city via the East Coast. Journey time is approximately 60-75 minutes.
- **Taxi:** A metered taxi to the city centre costs SGD 25-40, plus applicable surcharges (airport surcharge of SGD 5 during peak hours, SGD 3 off-peak).
- **Private Hire/Grab:** Book through the Grab app. Typical fare to the city is SGD 20-35.
- **Airport Shuttle:** Available for selected hotels.

### Visa Requirements
Citizens of many countries receive visa-free entry for 30 to 90 days. Check with the Immigration and Checkpoints Authority (ICA) for the latest requirements. All visitors must complete an SG Arrival Card (electronic) up to three days before arrival.

### Cruises
Singapore is a major cruise hub. The Marina Bay Cruise Centre and Singapore Cruise Centre at HarbourFront serve regional and international cruise lines.

## Climate and Best Time to Visit

Singapore is located just 137 kilometres north of the equator and experiences a tropical rainforest climate.

### Temperature
- Average daily temperature: 27-31 degrees Celsius (80-88 Fahrenheit)
- Nighttime lows: 24-26 degrees Celsius
- Humidity: 70-90%

### Rainfall Seasons
- **Northeast Monsoon (December to March):** Wettest period with frequent, prolonged afternoon and evening rain. December and January are the rainiest months.
- **Inter-Monsoon (April to May):** Hot and humid with occasional thunderstorms, mainly in the afternoon.
- **Southwest Monsoon (June to September):** Drier period with lighter rain and occasional Sumatran squalls in the early morning.
- **Inter-Monsoon (October to November):** Transition period with afternoon thunderstorms.

### What to Pack
- Light, breathable clothing (cotton or moisture-wicking fabrics)
- Comfortable walking shoes
- Umbrella or compact rain jacket
- Light cardigan or sweater for heavily air-conditioned indoor spaces
- Sunscreen and sunglasses
- Reusable water bottle (tap water is safe to drink in Singapore)

## Health and Safety

### Healthcare
Singapore has world-class healthcare facilities. Major public hospitals include Singapore General Hospital, National University Hospital, and Tan Tock Seng Hospital. Private hospitals such as Mount Elizabeth and Gleneagles are also excellent. Travel insurance with medical coverage is strongly recommended.

### Vaccinations
No mandatory vaccinations are required for entry from most countries. A Yellow Fever vaccination certificate is required if arriving from or transiting through a Yellow Fever-endemic country.

### Water Safety
Tap water in Singapore meets WHO drinking water standards and is safe to drink directly.

### Dengue and Mosquitoes
Dengue fever is present in Singapore. Use insect repellent, especially during the wetter months. Wear long sleeves and pants in the evening if visiting parks or nature reserves.

### Emergency Numbers
- Police: 999
- Ambulance / Fire: 995
- Non-emergency ambulance: 1777

## Money Matters

### Currency
The Singapore Dollar (SGD) is the official currency. Notes come in denominations of SGD 2, 5, 10, 50, 100, 1000, and 10000. Coins are in 5, 10, 20, and 50 cent denominations.

### Payment Methods
Singapore is largely cashless. Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard, American Express) are accepted at most establishments. Contactless payments (PayWave, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Samsung Pay) are widespread. Some hawker centres and small shops may be cash-only, though even this is changing with the introduction of SGQR (unified QR code payment).

### Money Exchange
Best rates are found at licensed money changers rather than banks or hotels. Recommended locations:
- **The Arcade** at Raffles Place (widely regarded as having the best rates)
- **Mustafa Centre** in Little India (24 hours)
- **Lucky Plaza** on Orchard Road
- **People's Park Complex** in Chinatown

## Connectivity

### SIM Cards and Mobile Data
Prepaid tourist SIM cards are available at Changi Airport from Singtel, StarHub, and M1. Typical tourist SIM options include:
- **Singtel hi!Tourist SIM:** SGD 15 for 100GB data (5 days)
- **StarHub Travel SIM:** SGD 12 for 100GB data (5 days)
- **M1 Tourist SIM:** SGD 15 for 100GB data (7 days)

eSIM options are also available from these providers and international services like Airalo.

### Free Wi-Fi
Wireless@SG provides free Wi-Fi at major public spaces including MRT stations, libraries, hawker centres, and shopping malls. Connect to the "Wireless@SGx" network. Registration with a local mobile number or email is required.

### Postal Services
SingPost provides postal services. Post offices are located in shopping malls and neighbourhood centres. Stamps for international postcards cost SGD 0.50-1.50.

## Shopping

### Major Shopping Areas
- **Orchard Road:** Singapore's main shopping street with over 20 malls including ION Orchard, Ngee Ann City/Takashimaya, and Paragon.
- **Marina Bay Sands Shoppes:** Luxury brands and high-end retail along the waterfront.
- **Bugis Junction / Bugis+:** Mix of local and international brands, youth-oriented fashion.
- **VivoCity (HarbourFront):** Singapore's largest shopping mall, gateway to Sentosa.
- **Mustafa Centre (Little India):** A 24-hour department store selling everything from electronics to spices.

### GST Refund (Tourist Refund Scheme)
Tourists can claim a refund on the 9% Goods and Services Tax (GST) for purchases of SGD 100 or more at participating retailers. Use the eTRS (Electronic Tourist Refund Scheme) at the airport before departure. Look for "Tax Free Shopping" signs in stores.

## Cultural Etiquette

### General Guidelines
- Remove shoes before entering someone's home or a place of worship.
- Use your right hand to give or receive items (especially when interacting with Malay or Indian communities).
- Avoid touching someone's head, which is considered sacred in some cultures.
- Public displays of affection are generally modest.
- Queue politely. Singaporeans take queuing seriously, including the practice of "choping" (reserving) seats at hawker centres using tissue packets.

### Visiting Places of Worship
- Dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees).
- Remove shoes before entering temples and mosques.
- Some mosques provide robes for visitors.
- Ask permission before taking photographs.
- At Hindu temples, walk clockwise around the main shrine.

### Dining Etiquette
- It is common to share dishes at the table.
- At hawker centres, find a seat first, then order.
- Use the tissue packet to "chope" (reserve) your seat.
- Utensils vary: chopsticks for Chinese food, fork and spoon for Malay and Indian food, hands for some traditional dishes (roti prata, banana leaf rice).

## Holidays and Events

### Public Holidays (2024-2025)
- New Year's Day (January 1)
- Chinese New Year (January/February - two days)
- Good Friday (March/April)
- Hari Raya Puasa (Eid al-Fitr - date varies)
- Labour Day (May 1)
- Vesak Day (May)
- Hari Raya Haji (Eid al-Adha - date varies)
- National Day (August 9)
- Deepavali (October/November)
- Christmas Day (December 25)

### Major Events
- **Singapore Grand Prix (September/October):** Formula 1 night race around the Marina Bay street circuit.
- **Singapore Food Festival (July):** Celebrating Singapore's diverse culinary heritage.
- **Great Singapore Sale (June-August):** Islandwide retail promotions.
- **Singapore Night Festival (August):** Art installations and performances in the Bras Basah-Bugis precinct.
- **Mid-Autumn Festival (September/October):** Lantern displays in Chinatown and Gardens by the Bay.
- **Christmas on A Great Street (November-December):** Light-up along Orchard Road.
