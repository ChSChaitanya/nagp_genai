# Singapore Travel Guide

Singapore is a city-state in Southeast Asia, known for its multicultural heritage, world-class food scene, lush greenery, and futuristic architecture. Despite its small size (about 733 square kilometres), Singapore packs an extraordinary range of experiences for travellers.

## Districts and Neighbourhoods

### Marina Bay and the Civic District
Marina Bay is the heart of modern Singapore. Key landmarks include Marina Bay Sands, the ArtScience Museum, the Esplanade Theatres on the Bay, and the Merlion Park. The Civic District features colonial-era buildings such as the National Gallery Singapore (formerly the Supreme Court and City Hall), the Asian Civilisations Museum, and the Victoria Theatre and Concert Hall. The area is ideal for evening walks along the waterfront, with the nightly Spectra light-and-water show at Marina Bay Sands.

### Chinatown
Singapore's Chinatown is a vibrant district blending heritage and modernity. Visit the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple, the Sri Mariamman Temple (Singapore's oldest Hindu temple), and the Chinatown Heritage Centre. The bustling street markets along Pagoda Street, Trengganu Street, and Smith Street offer souvenirs, traditional crafts, and hawker-style street food. The Maxwell Food Centre, located here, is one of the best hawker centres in the city.

### Little India
Little India is a sensory feast of colours, aromas, and sounds. Highlights include the Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple, the colourful Tan Teng Niah house, Mustafa Centre (a 24-hour shopping complex), and the Tekka Centre wet market and hawker centre. The area comes alive especially during Deepavali with dazzling light displays along Serangoon Road.

### Kampong Glam and Arab Street
Kampong Glam is Singapore's Malay-Arab heritage quarter. Visit the Sultan Mosque (Masjid Sultan), the Malay Heritage Centre, and stroll along Haji Lane for independent boutiques, street art, and hip cafes. Arab Street is known for textile shops and Middle Eastern restaurants.

### Orchard Road
Orchard Road is Singapore's premier shopping belt stretching over 2 kilometres. Major malls include ION Orchard, Ngee Ann City, Paragon, and Mandarin Gallery. The area also features the Istana (the official residence of the President, open to visitors on select public holidays) and the Singapore Botanic Gardens at its western end.

### Sentosa Island
Sentosa is Singapore's resort island, accessible by cable car, monorail, boardwalk, or bus. Attractions include Universal Studios Singapore, S.E.A. Aquarium, Adventure Cove Waterpark, Madame Tussauds, and the Skyline Luge. The island also has several beaches: Siloso Beach, Palawan Beach, and Tanjong Beach. Fort Siloso is a preserved coastal fort from World War II.

### Jurong and the West
The western part of Singapore offers nature and science attractions. Jurong Bird Park (now part of Bird Paradise at Mandai), the Science Centre Singapore, and the Chinese Garden and Japanese Garden provide a quieter experience away from the city centre.

### East Coast and Changi
The East Coast area is known for East Coast Park, a long stretch of beachfront with cycling paths, barbecue pits, and seafood restaurants. Changi Village offers a laid-back atmosphere with the Changi Museum (documenting World War II history) and Changi Beach. The Jewel Changi Airport is an attraction in itself, with the world's tallest indoor waterfall (Rain Vortex), gardens, shopping, and dining.

### Mandai and the North
Mandai is home to Singapore's wildlife parks: Singapore Zoo, Night Safari, River Wonders, and Bird Paradise. The Singapore Zoo is famous for its open-concept enclosures where animals roam in naturalistic habitats. Night Safari is the world's first nocturnal wildlife park. The nearby MacRitchie Reservoir has a popular treetop walk and hiking trails.

## Major Attractions

### Gardens by the Bay
Gardens by the Bay is a 101-hectare park featuring the iconic Supertree Grove, Cloud Forest, and Flower Dome conservatories. The Supertree Grove features 18 tree-like structures up to 50 metres tall, connected by the OCBC Skyway aerial walkway. The Cloud Forest houses the world's tallest indoor waterfall at 35 metres. The Flower Dome is the world's largest glass greenhouse. The garden's outdoor areas are free; conservatories require tickets (adult SGD 32, child SGD 18). Best visited in the evening for the Garden Rhapsody light show (7:45 PM and 8:45 PM daily).

### National Gallery Singapore
Housed in the former Supreme Court and City Hall buildings, the National Gallery Singapore is the largest visual arts institution in Southeast Asia, with over 8,000 artworks. Admission: SGD 20 for international visitors (free for Singapore citizens and permanent residents).

### Singapore Flyer
The Singapore Flyer is a giant observation wheel standing 165 metres tall, offering panoramic views of the city, Marina Bay, and even parts of Malaysia and Indonesia on clear days. A single rotation takes about 30 minutes.

### Merlion Park
The Merlion, Singapore's iconic half-lion half-fish statue, stands at the waterfront in Merlion Park. It is one of the most photographed landmarks in Singapore. The park offers excellent views of Marina Bay Sands and the city skyline.

### ArtScience Museum
Designed in the shape of a lotus flower, the ArtScience Museum at Marina Bay Sands hosts rotating exhibitions that blend art, science, technology, and culture. Past exhibitions have featured teamLab, NASA space exploration, and Da Vinci.

## Local Transportation

### MRT (Mass Rapid Transit)
Singapore's MRT system is the backbone of public transportation. It covers most of the island with six lines: North-South (Red), East-West (Green), North-East (Purple), Circle (Yellow), Downtown (Blue), and Thomson-East Coast (Brown). Trains run from approximately 5:30 AM to midnight. Fares range from SGD 0.92 to SGD 2.20 per trip using an EZ-Link card or SimplyGo contactless bank card. An MRT station is rarely more than a 10-minute walk away in central areas.

### Public Buses
Singapore has an extensive bus network operated by SBS Transit and SMRT. Buses run from around 5:30 AM to midnight (some NightRider services run later on weekends). The same EZ-Link card used for MRT works on buses. Fares are distance-based and generally SGD 0.92 to SGD 2.17.

### Taxis and Ride-Hailing
Taxis are widely available and metered. Flag-down fare starts at SGD 4.00 to SGD 4.80. Surcharges apply during peak hours, midnight, and from the airport. Grab (the dominant ride-hailing app in Southeast Asia) and Gojek are popular alternatives. A typical ride from Changi Airport to the city centre costs SGD 25-40.

### Singapore Tourist Pass
The Singapore Tourist Pass offers unlimited rides on MRT and basic bus services. Available in 1-day (SGD 22), 2-day (SGD 29), and 3-day (SGD 34) options. This can be purchased at TransitLink counters in selected MRT stations.

### Walking
Singapore is a walkable city, especially in the central areas. Covered walkways connect many MRT stations to nearby buildings and attractions. The Marina Bay waterfront loop is a popular walking route of approximately 3.5 kilometres.

## Cultural and Practical Travel Tips

### Language
Singapore has four official languages: English, Mandarin Chinese, Malay, and Tamil. English is the primary language of business, education, and government. Signage, menus, and public announcements are predominantly in English. Singlish (Singapore English Creole) is widely spoken informally.

### Currency
The currency is the Singapore Dollar (SGD). Credit and debit cards are accepted almost everywhere. Contactless payments via PayWave, Apple Pay, and Google Pay are common. ATMs are widely available. Money changers in areas like Mustafa Centre (Little India) and The Arcade (Raffles Place) offer competitive rates.

### Climate
Singapore has a tropical rainforest climate: hot and humid year-round with temperatures between 25 and 33 degrees Celsius. Rain can occur any time, often as short, heavy afternoon thunderstorms. The wettest months are November to January (northeast monsoon). Carry an umbrella or light rain jacket at all times.

### Safety
Singapore is one of the safest cities in the world. Violent crime is extremely rare. Petty theft is uncommon but standard precautions are advised. Emergency services: Police 999, Ambulance/Fire 995.

### Laws and Regulations
Singapore enforces strict laws. Chewing gum is restricted (import for personal use is allowed in small quantities). Littering, jaywalking, and smoking in non-designated areas carry fines. Drug offences carry severe penalties including the death penalty. Always check local regulations.

### Tipping
Tipping is not customary in Singapore. Hotels and restaurants typically add a 10% service charge and 7% GST (increasing to 9% from 2024). Tipping taxi drivers is not expected, though rounding up the fare is appreciated.

### Electricity
Singapore uses Type G power plugs (British three-pin) at 230V/50Hz. Visitors from countries using different plug types will need an adapter.

### Connectivity
Singapore has excellent mobile connectivity and widespread free Wi-Fi. Tourists can purchase prepaid SIM cards from providers like Singtel, StarHub, and M1 at Changi Airport or convenience stores. Free Wi-Fi is available through Wireless@SG at most public venues.

## Food and Local Experiences

### Hawker Centres
Hawker centres are the soul of Singapore's food culture. These open-air food courts offer a vast array of local dishes at affordable prices (SGD 3-8 per dish). Must-visit hawker centres include:
- **Maxwell Food Centre** (Chinatown) - Famous for Tian Tian Hainanese Chicken Rice.
- **Lau Pa Sat** (CBD) - A Victorian-era cast-iron structure serving satay and local dishes.
- **Old Airport Road Food Centre** - Considered one of the best by locals with over 150 stalls.
- **Tiong Bahru Market** - Known for chwee kueh and local breakfast items.
- **Chomp Chomp Food Centre** (Serangoon) - Popular evening-only hawker centre.

### Must-Try Dishes
- **Hainanese Chicken Rice** - Singapore's unofficial national dish. Poached chicken served with fragrant rice, chilli sauce, and ginger paste.
- **Laksa** - A spicy coconut curry noodle soup with prawns, fish cake, and cockles. The Katong variety is especially famous.
- **Char Kway Teow** - Stir-fried flat rice noodles with prawns, Chinese sausage, egg, and bean sprouts.
- **Chilli Crab** - Large mud crabs cooked in a sweet-spicy tomato-chilli sauce, served with fried mantou buns.
- **Satay** - Grilled meat skewers (chicken, beef, or mutton) served with peanut sauce, rice cakes, and onion-cucumber relish.
- **Roti Prata** - A flaky South Indian flatbread served with curry. Available plain or with fillings like egg, cheese, or banana.
- **Kaya Toast** - Toasted bread with coconut jam (kaya) and butter, traditionally eaten for breakfast with soft-boiled eggs and kopi (local coffee).
- **Bak Kut Teh** - Pork ribs simmered in a peppery herbal broth. The Teochew-style is lighter with more pepper, while the Hokkien-style is darker and more herbal.
- **Ice Kachang** - A shaved ice dessert topped with red beans, jelly, sweet corn, and colourful syrup.
- **Nasi Lemak** - Coconut rice with sambal, fried anchovies, peanuts, egg, and a side dish such as fried chicken or fish.

### Fine Dining and Unique Experiences
Singapore has a thriving fine dining scene. Notable Michelin-starred restaurants include Odette, Les Amis, and Burnt Ends. For a unique experience, try dining at CE LA VI (atop Marina Bay Sands), 1-Altitude (rooftop bar), or take a Singapore River Cruise dinner.

### Coffee Culture
Traditional kopitiam (coffee shops) serve kopi - local-style coffee brewed with a sock filter using robusta beans roasted with butter and sugar. Common orders:
- Kopi = coffee with condensed milk
- Kopi-O = black coffee with sugar
- Kopi-C = coffee with evaporated milk and sugar
- Kopi-O-Kosong = black coffee without sugar

Specialty third-wave coffee shops are also plentiful, especially in Tiong Bahru, Kampong Glam, and Everton Park.
